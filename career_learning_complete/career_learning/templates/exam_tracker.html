📆
Start 6 Months Early
Finish NCERT first, then H.C. Verma, JD Lee, and RD Sharma. 6–8 hours daily study with subject rotation.
📝
Mock Tests Every Week
Full mock every Sunday. Analyse wrong answers — most students lose 20–30 marks due to careless errors, not lack of knowledge.
🔢
Numerical = Free Marks
Section B has no negative marking. Always attempt all 10 numerical questions — rough estimates can also score full marks.
⚡
Time Management
Target 2 min/question. Ideal split: Physics 60 min, Chemistry 55 min, Maths 65 min. Skip uncertain MCQs (–1 penalty).

📖
Concept Over Formula
JEE Advanced is conceptual. Understand derivations and the "why" behind every concept — memorising formulas alone won't work.
🧩
Careful with Multi-Correct
Selecting a wrong option gives negative marks. If even one option is uncertain, it's safer to skip the entire question.
📊
Solve 15 Years PYQs
Previous Year Questions from 2008–2024 are the best resource. JEE Advanced often tests similar concepts in different disguises.
🧠
HC Verma + Irodov
For Physics, HC Verma builds concepts and Irodov builds problem-solving. For Chemistry, JD Lee + Narendra Awasthi for PYQ practice.

📗
NCERT is Everything
90%+ NEET questions come directly from NCERT Biology. Read every line, every table, every diagram caption — word for word.
🔁
Revise 3 Times Minimum
First read to understand. Second read to memorise. Third read to retain. Use flashcards for taxonomy, organ systems, and hormones.
🧬
Biology = 50% of Exam
Score 340+/360 in Bio — it can compensate for average Physics/Chemistry. Prioritise Biology with 60% of your study time.
📋
Practice OMR Sheets
NEET is pen-and-paper. Practice carefully filling OMR bubbles under time pressure — wrong bubbling costs 4 marks per question.

📰
Read Newspaper Daily
The Hindu or Indian Express every day. GK + Current Affairs carry 28–32 marks. Note important SC judgements and constitutional amendments.
📜
Legal Reasoning = Highest Weight
35–39 questions — the biggest section. No prior law knowledge needed. Simply apply the given principle logically to the fact situation.
⏱️
Reading Speed is Key
CLAT is entirely passage-based. Practice reading 400-word passages in under 3 minutes. Active reading (underline key points) is essential.
🔢
Never Skip Quant
Only Class 10-level Maths. Worth 13–17 marks — easy to score. Practise percentages, ratios, and data interpretation daily.

📚
VARC: Read Voraciously
Read editorials from The Hindu, The Economist, and HBR. RC passages in CAT are complex — reading speed and comprehension is the key differentiator.
🧩
DILR: Identify Set Type Fast
First 2 minutes: scan all sets, pick the 2 easiest. Don't get stuck on a tough set — a wrong choice wastes 20 minutes and kills your percentile.
🔢
QA: Shortcuts Over Formulae
Master approximation and Vedic Maths. 90% of QA can be solved faster with estimation than exact calculation.
📊
Sectional Percentile Matters
IIMs check each section separately. Even a 99 overall percentile won't help if you score below 70 in any single section.

Sketch Every Day
Draw everyday objects — chairs, bottles, phones. Focus on proportions, light/shadow, and confident quick lines. 30 min daily sketching is non-negotiable.

Train Visual Observation

📗
NCERT is Sufficient
CUET domain questions are mostly NCERT-based. Thorough NCERT revision + past papers = 90%+ score. No need for extra coaching material.
⏱️
Speed is Critical
45 minutes for 45 questions = 1 min per question. Practice with strict timers. Skip & return strategy works best here.
📊
Normalization Effect
Scores are normalized across shifts. Attempt maximum correct answers — even a few extra rights can significantly boost your normalized score.
🎯
Subject Selection Matters
Choose up to 6 subjects aligned with your target college. SRCC requires Accountancy + Economics + English as minimum. Plan subject combo carefully.
